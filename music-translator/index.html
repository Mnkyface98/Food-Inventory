Carnatic ↔ Western Note Translator

Convert Carnatic swaras (S R G M P D N) to Western note names, and back — so a phrase played on veena, violin or voice can be read out on piano, guitar or bass.

Sa is the movable "do" of Carnatic music — pick whatever note your instrument's Sa is tuned to.

Carnatic → Western

Notes separated by commas and/or spaces, e.g. S, R2, G3, M1, P, D2, N3, S'. A trailing ' raises an octave (tara sthayi), a trailing _ lowers one (mandra sthayi). A bare letter with no number (e.g. R) is read as its natural-scale variant. Wrap each phrase in । … ॥ to keep multi-line pieces grouped in the output — or paste text without them and it's treated as one phrase.

Western → Carnatic

Notes separated by commas and/or spaces, e.g. C4, D4, E4, F4, G4, A4, B4, C5. Sharps use #, flats use b. Octave number is optional (defaults to the tonic's octave).

Swarasthana chart

The 16 Carnatic swara names collapse onto 12 semitones (like Western chromatic notes) — R3/G2 and D3/N2 are enharmonic pairs.

About uploading sheet music

.txt files load exactly as written — no recognition involved, just drops the file's text into the box for you to translate.

Photo uploads run OCR (text recognition, via Tesseract.js) in your browser. That works reasonably well on a clear photo of printed swara text (like a notation booklet) — always review the result before translating, since OCR makes mistakes, especially on handwriting or low-contrast scans.

It cannot read actual Western staff notation — a photo of notes sitting on a five-line staff. That's a different, much harder problem (optical music recognition, not text OCR), and isn't supported here. For staff notation, type the note names in by hand as you read them off the page.

This is a note-name translator, not a full raga/gamaka transcriber — it maps individual swara pitches to their Western equivalents relative to your chosen Sa. Ornamentation (gamakas) and microtonal shading used in Carnatic performance aren't represented in Western 12-tone notation.
