Carnatic ↔ Western Note Translator

Convert Carnatic swaras (S R G M P D N) to Western note names, and back — so a phrase played on veena, violin or voice can be read out on piano, guitar or bass.

BPM Sa is the movable "do" of Carnatic music — pick whatever note your instrument's Sa is tuned to. The tambura drones on Sa continuously until stopped — it follows tonic changes live.

Carnatic → Western

Notes separated by commas and/or spaces, e.g. S, R2, G3, M1, P, D2, N3, S'. A trailing ' raises an octave (tara sthayi), a trailing _ lowers one (mandra sthayi). A bare letter with no number (e.g. R) is read as its natural-scale variant. Wrap each phrase in । … ॥ to keep multi-line pieces grouped in the output — or paste text without them and it's treated as one phrase. Paste as much as you like — there's no length limit on this box.

Western → Carnatic

Notes separated by commas and/or spaces, e.g. C4, D4, E4, F4, G4, A4, B4, C5. Sharps use #, flats use b. Octave number is optional (defaults to the tonic's octave). You can also paste (or upload) ASCII guitar/bass tab — 6 lines for guitar, 4 for bass, each starting with a string label and |, e.g. e|--0--3--| — it's detected automatically and converted the same way.

Swarasthana chart

The 16 Carnatic swara names collapse onto 12 semitones (like Western chromatic notes) — R3/G2 and D3/N2 are enharmonic pairs.

About uploading sheet music

Document uploads (.txt, .md, .doc, .docx, .pdf) load the file's text straight into the box. No recognition involved for .txt/.md; .docx and .pdf have their text extracted in your browser (via Mammoth.js and PDF.js). Legacy .doc has no reliable browser parser, so it gets a best-effort text scrape instead — always review it before translating. A scanned (image-only) PDF has no extractable text, so that still needs the photo/OCR path below.

Photo uploads run OCR (text recognition, via Tesseract.js) in your browser. That works reasonably well on a clear photo of printed swara text (like a notation booklet) — always review the result before translating, since OCR makes mistakes, especially on handwriting or low-contrast scans.

It cannot read actual Western staff notation — a photo of notes sitting on a five-line staff. That's a different, much harder problem (optical music recognition, not text OCR), and isn't supported here. For staff notation, type the note names in by hand as you read them off the page.

This is a note-name translator, not a full raga/gamaka transcriber — it maps individual swara pitches to their Western equivalents relative to your chosen Sa. Ornamentation (gamakas) and microtonal shading used in Carnatic performance aren't represented in Western 12-tone notation.
